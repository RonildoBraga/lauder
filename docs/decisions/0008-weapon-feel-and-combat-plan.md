# ADR 0008 — Weapon feel, and a plan for combat

**Status:** Accepted\
**Date:** 22 November 2026

**Later change:** [ADR 0014](0014-continuous-combat-arena.md) supersedes the retained hostile red aiming line and the combat yard's finite encounter/fallback policy. This record preserves the earlier phase's assignment and delivery evidence.


**Current scope:** The bridge-only reset retired the non-bridge experiments described here. Shared rules remain only where the bridge uses them; this record preserves the earlier decisions and measurements, not an assignment to restore deleted systems. See [ADR 0017](0017-bridge-only-reset.md) and the current [architecture](../architecture.md).

## Context

On 21 November 2026, after playing [ADR 0007](0007-aiming-and-shot-feedback.md)'s shooting, the maintainer asked to take combat further: **“I can see the improvements, but I think we need to take the combat system further to make it feel more realistic, immersive, and engaging.”** The request named these changes:
- **No aim line:** “when aiming, we shouldn't display a visible line indicating where the bullet will travel. Instead, the character should physically aim their weapon in the intended direction, with natural aiming animations and weapon behaviour.”
- **Haptic feedback:** “Add controller vibrations when firing weapons, with different feedback depending on the weapon type.”
- **Different weapons:** “Allow the player and companion characters to use firearms, with different weapon types, characteristics, animations, and shooting effects.”
- **Cover system:** “Characters should be able to move into cover, peek out, aim, and shoot while remaining protected.”
- **Companion combat:** “Companions should actively participate in combat, using their own weapons, seeking cover, and responding intelligently to enemy threats.”
- **Realistic shooting mechanics:** “Improve weapon handling, recoil, muzzle flashes, bullet impacts, sound effects, and enemy reactions to make combat feel more convincing.”

The maintainer named Tom Clancy's The Division 2 as the reference, to adapt to the top-down camera and the phone's controls “without making the game unnecessarily complicated”. Before any implementation, the maintainer asked for research, a comparison with the game as it is, a proposal, the phone's limits and a phased plan, and to focus “on getting the core combat experience right before adding more complexity”.

The research found:
- **The enemies give The Division 2 its depth.** Each of its nine enemy types poses a readable problem, cover protects only from the side it faces, and armour visibly breaks ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-the-enemy-ai-of-the-division-2)).
- **Its controls are what players criticise.** One button carries cover, the run between covers and the roll, and it feels sticky. Ubisoft's phone version, The Division Resurgence (2026), adds crowded buttons and cover that sometimes does not engage ([Gamingtrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/the-division-resurgence-review/)). None of the top-down games examined snaps to cover and peeks; the closest crouch behind cover with no button.
- **Invisible aim help backfires.** A top-down shooter whose aim moved by itself fixed it with a cue that shows when the help takes over ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/everything-i-learned-about-dual-stick-shooter-controls)).
- **Vibration is open ground.** Neither game is documented as using controller vibration or DualSense triggers. Apple asks for one pattern per cause and a way to turn vibration off ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)).
- **Phones limit the frame rate.** Resurgence guarantees only 30 fps on phones with the baseline iPhone's chip ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/the-division-resurgence/performance-mobile)); the combat yard holds 60.

On 22 November 2026, asked whether to start the first phase with the recommended choices, the maintainer answered **“Start Phase 1”**, which settled:

| Question | The maintainer's choice |
| --- | --- |
| Can shots miss? | Yes, through a visible spread |
| Where do the sounds come from? | Generated by a script in the repository |
| What happens to the hostiles' red aim line? | It stays as their warning |

## Decision

### 1. Combat improves in five phases

Each phase is built and tested in the Simulator. After build 20's delivery, the maintainer first authorised continued Simulator development ([ADR 0010](0010-squad-weapons.md)), then assigned finishing the remaining combat phases, reviewed integration and TestFlight delivery without repeated milestone prompts ([ADR 0011](0011-companion-combat.md)). Physical checks and maintainer judgement remain separate evidence; they are not inferred from Simulator success and do not pause this assigned sequence at every milestone. Cover, companion and enemy changes start in the combat yard, as [ADR 0005](0005-squad-combat-experiment.md)'s did. The weapon's feel applies everywhere, as ADR 0007's changes did. Each fight becomes deeper, but there are no more story encounters: the [story bible](../story/story-bible.md) still keeps combat occasional after the bridge.

This record decided the first phase. Later assignments are recorded separately; the numeric settings remain tunable prototype details rather than permanent character canon:
1. **The shot:** this record.
2. **Cover for the leader:** now assigned and recorded in [ADR 0009](0009-leader-cover.md).
   - no cover button: standing near a block between the leader and a threat is cover;
   - firing rises over a low block or steps out at a tall block's edge, and the leader is exposed while firing;
   - cover protects only from the side it faces;
   - a shield on the ground shows it, and turns red when a threat can see the leader.
3. **The squad's weapons:** now assigned and built in the Simulator prototype recorded in [ADR 0010](0010-squad-weapons.md). Laura and Tom fire their own weapons when they lead, since the story bible makes each “useful as leader and capable of ordinary weapon defence”. Each member keeps magazine and reload state across roles, reloads are automatic, and companions' shots use spread. One shared ammunition total remains.
4. **Companion combat:** implemented and delivered in TestFlight 0.1.0 (21), as recorded in [ADR 0011](0011-companion-combat.md). Companions use the shared cover rule and shields, prefer the leader's valid target and reconsider cover when flanked while retaining their orders.
5. **Enemies that fight back:** implemented and delivered in TestFlight 0.1.0 (21), as recorded in [ADR 0012](0012-tactical-enemies.md). The yard tries cover-seeking and flanking shooters and a fixed, warned grenade thrower. Fights get harder through behaviour, not by adding hits.

Unless the maintainer widens the [prototype's scope](../bridge-prototype-plan.md#outside-the-first-playable), these stay out:
- armour plates and weak points, which are a health system;
- spoken callouts, which are voice acting;
- rigged animation, which is finished character art;
- Laura's and Tom's proposed signatures.

### 2. The pose shows the aim

- **The line goes:** ADR 0007's amber aim line is removed.
- **Pose:** with the carbine drawn, Miki holds it low across her body. Aiming or firing raises it to her shoulder, pointing the way she faces. It lowers again 0.8 s after she last aims or fires.
- **Turning:** Miki turns at up to two full turns a second, 720° a second, never snapping. That applies whether she aims, fires or walks. Companions keep their 540° a second.
- **Shots leave along her facing:**
  - With the right stick, she turns towards its aim and fires the way she faces. The 8° help works around her facing.
  - Without the stick, as on touch, Fire turns her towards the assisted target and fires once she faces within 8° of it.
- **The help stays visible:** the red disc still marks the hostile her next shot would hit.
- **Recoil:** every shot kicks the weapon back and up, and rocks the shooter back a little, whoever fires. It settles in about a tenth of a second.
- **Muzzle:** her streaks and flashes start at the carbine's muzzle.
- **No rig:** the figures have no skeleton, so a pose moves the whole figure and its weapon, not limbs.

### 3. Shots spread

Each shot goes within a cone around where it is aimed: the weapon's spread. For the carbine:

| Spread | Carbine |
| --- | --- |
| At rest | 0.4° either side |
| Each shot adds | 1.6°, up to 6° |
| Narrows by | 2.5° a second |
| Moving adds | up to 2.5°, at full speed |
| Turning adds | up to 2°, at full turning speed |

- **What it means:** standing still, the first shot of a short burst lands within 10 cm at the carbine's 14 m reach. A long burst, or firing on the move, scatters, and at range it can miss a figure, which a shot must pass within 0.35 m of.
- **What shows it:** each shot's streak, the only sign of the spread.
- **Never first:** if a scattered shot would meet someone who is not yet a target, it flies straight instead.
- **First-phase boundary:** this phase left companions' shots hitting what they aim at, and a hostile's shot hitting when its line is clear at the end of its wind-up. [ADR 0010](0010-squad-weapons.md)'s phase 3 applies spread to companions too; [ADR 0012](0012-tactical-enemies.md)'s assigned enemy work adds tactical positions and warned grenades in the yard.

### 4. Enemies react

- **A hit** rocks the figure away from the shot, beside the flash on its body. Members of the party react to a hostile's hit the same way.
- **A hit during a wind-up** delays the shot by 0.2 s, by at most 0.5 s for each wind-up. Only Disrupt cancels it.
- **A downed hostile** falls away from the shot over 0.4 s, instead of dropping at once.

### 5. Sound

- **What is heard:**
  - a shot from the carbine, Laura's burst weapon, Tom's scatter weapon and the hostiles' rifle;
  - a strike on a body;
  - a strike on a block;
  - an empty click.
- **Where they come from:** `apple/scripts/make-sounds.py` generates the WAV files using only Python's standard library, and they are committed. `scripts/check` confirms that they match what the script makes. Recordings can later replace them under the same names, with no change to code.
- **Where they play:** each plays from where it happens, panned across the screen and quieter the farther it is from the camera's target. At most eight play at once, and the oldest gives way.
- **The audio session is ambient:** the Silent switch mutes the game, and other audio keeps playing.

### 6. Vibration

- **Which device:** playing by touch, the phone vibrates. With a controller, the controller vibrates and the phone stays still.
- **Patterns:** a firm tap for each carbine shot, a sharp, heavier jolt when the leader is hit, and a light tick for an empty click. Phase 3 now gives Laura's and Tom's player-fired weapons distinct patterns, recorded in [ADR 0010](0010-squad-weapons.md); their physical feel remains unverified.
- **DualSense:** in this phase, R2 resists like a trigger's break while Miki can fire. [ADR 0010](0010-squad-weapons.md) extends it to distinct settings for all three weapons and clears resistance during automatic reloads. It goes slack while paused or outside play.
- **The switch:** the pause menu's **Vibration** choice turns all of it off and on again.
  - It starts on.
  - It carries over to the other level.
  - It is not kept between launches, like the other preferences.

## Consequences

- **ADR 0007:** the aim line goes, and its right-stick aim, help, streaks, effects and lens stay.
- **Turning:** the leader no longer turns instantly, including while exploring.
- **The yard is a little easier:** companions' hits delay wind-ups too, so the shooters fire less often while the companions engage them. The yard's scripted playtest no longer expects both shooters to be standing when the party falls back.
- **Ammunition:** missed shots spend rounds. The bridge gives 12 rounds and the stranger 6 more, against three hits for its two hostiles; the bridge's playtests check that this is still enough.
- **The repository:** it gains sound files and their generator.
- **Starting values:** every value here can be tuned in play.

## How it is judged

Simulator playtests come first. The following judgement and physical checks remain for play on the baseline iPhone with the PS5 controller and with touch. The current continuous assignment permits reviewed integration and TestFlight delivery while those results remain explicitly unverified:
- Without the line, can you tell where Miki aims, and does each shot feel solid?
- Does the spread feel fair, with the first shots landing and long bursts scattering?
- Do the enemies' reactions read?
- Do the sounds and vibrations feel right? Do the controller's vibration and the DualSense trigger work on the iPhone?
- Does the yard still hold 60 fps?

## Options considered

- **An aim line only while actively aiming,** as the research suggested: the maintainer asked for none.
- **Shots that never miss,** with recoil only for show.
- **Free CC0 recordings:** more realistic, but every file's source and licence must be tracked.
- **A raised-weapon pose and a glint** in place of the hostiles' red line: the line is the 1.5 s warning that leaves time to Disrupt or take cover.
- **Firing along the stick's aim while the figure is still turning:** the streak would leave the gun at an angle.
- **Shaking the screen for recoil:** it reads poorly from a high camera.

## What this does not settle

- Later phases are decided in their own records: phase 2 in [ADR 0009](0009-leader-cover.md), phase 3 in [ADR 0010](0010-squad-weapons.md), phase 4 in [ADR 0011](0011-companion-combat.md) and phase 5 in [ADR 0012](0012-tactical-enemies.md). All five are now implemented and delivered; the [prototype plan](../bridge-prototype-plan.md#verification-and-performance) owns the current validation and remaining physical checks rather than this historical first-phase decision.
- Magazines and reloads, subsequently assigned in phase 3 with one shared ammunition total.
- Aiming by hand on touch, such as a drag on Fire.
