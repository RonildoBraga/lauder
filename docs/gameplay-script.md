## The game we are building

One human player directs up to three characters travelling together. The selected character leads; the others follow through the same physical world. Switching changes who the player controls, not the group's progress, belongings or relationships.

The proposed trio is Miki, who understands systems and careful salvage; Laura, who is agile and reads access routes; and Tom, whose strength and load-handling experience offer another approach. Their abilities are understandable before the player knows their histories. Internal synthetic replacements and biological changes belong to the world, but this slice needs no medical exposition or visible machinery in anyone's body.

They travel together through a ruined future, in which safe water, maintained equipment and current route information matter. Why each of them is crossing, and how they came to travel together, is revealed gradually. Their deeper connections emerge through what they recognise and choose to do.

## The Brooklyn Bridge opening

The game begins with Miki, Tom and Laura together at the Brooklyn end of the bridge, heading towards Manhattan through a post-apocalyptic New York. It is built as a greybox, with simple shapes and colours for the layout, the encounters and the story's progress. Its look is moving to the frozen low-poly [visual direction](visual-direction.md), with the bridge's road and the river buried under snow. [ADR 0004](decisions/0004-brooklyn-bridge-opening.md) sets out how it is built.

### 1. Together on the bridge (M1, built)

The three start together at the Brooklyn end, Miki leading and Tom and Laura in the Close formation behind her. On 26 November 2026 the maintainer simplified the opening so that nobody has to be met on the way: **“I want to simplify the starting of the game so all the 3 companions will start together so no need to meet Tom and Laura later in the game”**.

Snow has buried the bridge's road, everything left on it and the river under it: a flat white plain, with the bridge 644 m from end to end and only its two granite towers standing above the snow, at their real spacing, 486 m apart, and at their real size, with tall arches the old roadways pass through. Nothing else stands on the snow but a line of thin poles with small rust flags every 25 m along both edges of the buried road, as on a polar route, so the way and the party's movement read on the white. The party keeps to that line, inside an invisible edge at the road's width, and walks straight across to the Manhattan end. A compass at the top of the screen points to Manhattan and counts down the metres to it.

The player learns to move, look, switch leader and try the formations. A few places show a short line the first time the leader reaches them, such as "Snow covers the road and everything left on it." and "The first tower. Its arches still stand." The camera stands high and far back, showing the buried road and the snow on both sides, and looks straight along the bridge, so pushing up walks towards Manhattan. While a tower stands between the camera and the party, it fades.

### 2. The guard at the first tower (M2)

A heavy, four-legged machine patrols the outgoing side of the first tower. Its dark faceted armour, red square eye and twin guns identify it as a different kind of threat. Miki notices that it is still guarding something; the scene does not explain who built it or why it remains there.

Quiet acquisition remains leader-only. Once engaged, the robot may retaliate against the most recent visible attacker within range. It turns toward that member at a limited rate, gives a named warning, stops and braces while tracking, then commits to one aim point. The warned member stays fixed through that cycle, even if the player switches leader or orders Defend; a committed burst no longer follows their position. The robot alternates three-round precision bursts with five-round fan sweeps, firing from its two guns, and recovers before the next cycle. There is no red aiming line or projected danger cone. Its named HUD cue, body and gun direction, planted pause and shots supply the warning. Actual barrel-origin shots meet the tower's stone; neither a gun nor a committed shot can ignore cover.

**Fire** uses the leader's weapon in hand, Miki's long rifle at first, and takes the assisted target in sight; the right stick gives manual aim, and R1 switches to the other weapon ([ADR 0021](decisions/0021-paired-weapons.md)). The robot is a valid target when engaged, without waiting for a human's deliberate warning miss. Build 32 used six-hit toughness, builds 33–35 required component dismantling, and builds 36–37 introduced shared health. The current assignment keeps that health pool and simplifies its opportunities to eye criticals, breakable legs and Tom's grenade, below. Each party member can take three hits, with at most one injury across the party from a robot burst. A third hit on any member fades the screen and retries from the existing first-tower checkpoint, resetting the encounter and weapon state.

The machine remains within its small guard area. The party can break sight, withdraw and return without resetting progress; each weapon retains its magazine and reloads automatically without a shared spare-round limit. The west arch leads to the authored safe exit beyond the whole guard envelope. That resolves the encounter without pretending that the robot was killed and cancels queued fire before the party walks on. It requires no attack.

**Delivered manual prototype (build 33):** The player reads and dismantles the machine rather than winning by firing any weapon into its armour:

1. Lead as Miki and use her long rifle to disable the sensor. That damage persists for the attempt.
2. Switch manually to Laura, use the tower and the machine's readable attack cycle to reach its side, and breach the coupling with her energy sword. Solid cover still stops the strike, and the breach persists.
3. Switch to Tom and use the minigun against the exposed reactor. Recovery opens a 4-second window; a breach made during recovery also grants the full window, even if the machine starts moving again. Twelve accepted core rounds across repeated windows disable the guard. Missing a window means using cover and waiting for the next, not repeating Miki's and Laura's work.

In that manual preview, the guard alternates three-round precision bursts with five-round fan cannon attacks, each with readable preparation and commitment. Wrong-role hits and closed armour give feedback without ordinary health damage. The two followers obey movement/hold orders but do not attack or complete steps automatically. Tom's shield is usable while he leads and drops on a switch. L1 / LB, Q or touch Next remains the only leader-cycle command; existing Fire, aim/lock, Swap and Roll supply the actions. The west bypass remains available throughout. [Strategic guard](strategic-guard.md) distinguishes this earlier mandatory sequence from the current shared-health contract; the prototype plan preserves the manual preview's validation and delivery record.

**Earlier companion revision, build 34:** The maintainer allows companions to complete weakpoint steps, then clarified that they may attack only after an explicit Attack order. The squad starts in Defend. D-pad/arrow Up orders Attack; Down orders Defend, stopping companion attacks and recalling them via nearby legal cover. Leader Fire alone does not authorise them. After Attack, once the fight is engaged, Miki may shoot the sensor, Laura breach a side coupling and Tom exploit the exposed core without changing leader for every contribution. Hold/Go retain movement priority until Defend explicitly clears them; later position orders remain defensive unless Attack is selected. The existing 8 m leash bounds tactical destinations. Formation/Follow still regroups temporarily without changing stance; retreat suppresses attacks, and Retry/Restart returns to Defend. Real rays, firing limits and vulnerability still apply. A quiet approach or west bypass must not become a fight because of a companion. [Companion autonomy](companion-autonomy.md) records the new ordinary-attack extension and its verification boundary.

**Earlier retaliation comparison, build 35:** An engaged guard may choose the most recent visible attacker between cycles. A named triangle highlight and one short two-note warning tell the player who is threatened. The machine turns toward that member through its full warning, retains the same member even if the player switches leader, and stops following their position once it commits the burst. Defend stops companion fire and recalls them; it does not erase a warned attack. The existing damage, patterns, timing, component sequence, cover and bypass remain the comparison baseline. The [retaliation contract](guard-retaliation.md) records the exact bounds; source and Simulator checks passed and build 35 is available to the existing testers, with phone acceptance still pending.

**Earlier damage revision, builds 36–37:** On 27 November the maintainer asked for every successful hit to cause damage and approved the proposal with **“Proceed with your recommendations”** (chat; no public permalink). Those previews added **1,500 shared health**, useful normal hits and optional **×3** matching-weapon bonuses on the sensor, coupling and four-second core windows. The mandatory chain and core-round count were removed, while that optional part sequence remained. These are the previous rules, not the current assignment.

**Current revision:** The guard still has **1,500 shared health**, and every actual damaging hit counts. Any gun that really strikes the visible eye receives **×3** damage; the eye remains available throughout the fight. Laura can concentrate three real sword contacts on one intact leg to detach it. Each missing leg reduces translation by **25%**. The current [locomotion follow-up](robot-eye-legs-grenades.md#leg-loss-locomotion-follow-up) adds slower physical turns and heavier remaining-foot steps; after all four are lost the hull settles and stops translating, retaining only a slow emergency pivot and warned attacks. Build 39 delivers that follow-up. There is no sensor-disable prerequisite or timed core window.

The new [stagger follow-up](robot-eye-legs-grenades.md#leg-break-stagger-follow-up) makes a newly lost leg interrupt unresolved robot shots, with a brief mechanical/body reaction and a complete fresh warning before more fire, while preserving the interrupted volley’s full recovery or the remainder of recovery already in progress. It does not undo injuries from earlier resolved shots, and ordinary hits cannot keep the robot staggered. This working implementation is not included in build 39.

**Crawler pack:** three small crawlers wait folded beside the guard and get up once its fight starts. Each walks round the stone towards a member and crouches with its sensor glowing for 0.6 s; its ram line is fixed from that moment, so stepping aside or rolling beats it. It then rams 3 m, stops, and stays exposed for a moment. One rifle or shotgun round downs a crawler, the aim help lowers shots to them, and a grenade catches a cluster. They never wind up over the guard's own attacks, and they go down when it does. The [crawler contract](crawler-pack.md) has the rules.

**Scout drones, combined revision delivered:** two small drones circle the guard about 3 m up, out of sword reach. Once its fight is engaged they take turns: a drone hovers and glows for a second, then dives along a committed line at the member it watched, fires a short burst, sweeps low past them and climbs back. Moving or taking cover beats the dive; the low pass is when the sword, the shotgun and level shots reach it, while the aim help and the lock reach it up high. Two solid hits bring one down, and both fall when the guard does. The source-verified warning-target correction keeps that warning with its named member’s last-seen point; another member requires a new complete warning. The [drone contract](scout-drones.md) and [combined delivery record](prototype-plan.md#build-43-combined-testflight-preview) record passed source/Simulator checks, verified TestFlight availability and remaining limits.

When Tom leads, Circle/B, keyboard C or touch **Grenade** makes a manual throw, with an initial **8-second recharge** and **10 m** maximum range. The committed arc stops on real scenery and explodes after landing; one cover-checked blast damages each enemy once, without an eye bonus or sword-leg progress. Companions never throw grenades automatically. The [robot contract](robot-eye-legs-grenades.md) owns tuning, resource and lifecycle details.

Normal applied damage stays white; eye criticals are red with another cue. Leg damage gains a small indicator before the full assembly drops into a noncolliding prop. Rapid gun contacts retain separate normal/critical totals, and feedback remains bounded across Pause/interruption/Retry/Restart. Under Attack, Miki/Tom prefer reachable eye shots and Laura an intact leg, with safe ordinary attacks as fallback. Defend remains cease-fire and recall; the player always owns the leader. The [damage contract](damage-feedback.md) owns health and feedback. Source and Simulator verification for the delivered eye/leg/grenade baseline passed on `303617b`; the newer locomotion has its own [verification record](prototype-plan.md#leg-loss-locomotion--in-development). Earlier recordings do not validate that follow-up.

### 3. Onwards (M4 and M5, built)

Beyond the guard, nothing more stops the three. The maintainer chose to keep the guard as the bridge's only fight, so the supply case's search, the knife attacker's ambush with Tom's shot and Laura's rescue with the medical kit are gone. The case still stands on the tower's outgoing west side, as scenery with nothing in it.

The three cross the second tower together to the Manhattan end, where the party stops for a short closing exchange and the objective reads "Manhattan, together.": the end of the opening. The formations of [ADR 0003](decisions/0003-squad-movement.md) are there from the start (M5). **Formation** cycles **Close**; **Spread**, a wider front; and **Column**, single file on the leader's route. Where a passage is too narrow for it, Spread pulls in or falls into single file, then spreads out again beyond; the buried bridge's arches leave room for it. The HUD names the formation, the closing exchange and a change of leader keep it, and Restart returns to Close. Position orders work from the start too: D-pad left/right sends that companion ahead; keyboard G/H or the touch ground menu sends Both or holds them. D-pad up/down now controls Attack/Defend. **Formation** reads **Follow** after a position order and calls everyone back. The closing exchange waits until all three are at the Manhattan end.

Lines stay provisional and brief. None of the scenes explains anyone's past beyond what the moment needs.

## What the opening has

The bridge is one level value: its scenery, starting party, spawns, trigger areas and encounters, while the session holds the party and the story's beat. Three [pawns](visual-direction.md#characters-and-interface) share session-owned leadership and Pause, leader cycling and an eased camera, and companions follow as a squad ([ADR 0003](decisions/0003-squad-movement.md) phases S1 to S3) rather than on the leader's exact path: in whichever formation the player chooses, or where an order sends them. The merged guard encounter has bounded patrol, a committed burst, real cover, retreat, a safe bypass and a retry; then comes the end of the opening. Every character can use their paired weapons as leader ([ADR 0021](decisions/0021-paired-weapons.md)), and the leader can dodge-roll through a burst ([ADR 0019](decisions/0019-dodge-roll.md)). The current [eye/leg/grenade extension](robot-eye-legs-grenades.md) builds on the delivered shared-health preview; its source and Simulator verification is recorded separately in the [prototype plan](prototype-plan.md#eye-legs-and-grenades--in-development). [Touch controls](touch-controls.md) give the phone a second input path.

## Readable controls and party feedback

Every input, controller, touch or keyboard, uses the same movement, action, switching and Pause rules, and the action also advances a conversation and confirms the pause menu's choice; the [input contract](prototype-plan.md#input-and-interaction-contract) lists the controls. Show the active character's name, the next switch target and the appropriate input hint. The status panel keeps to the essentials: the party and its formation always, a new objective or line for a few seconds, and a threat or the active weapon's readiness while they matter. It shows the weapon in hand: its loaded rounds or a reload countdown, how long Tom's dome lasts, or just the sword; outside fights it labels free practice. Three differently coloured pawns are sufficient, with text so colour alone carries no essential information; no new art is a prerequisite.

Switching is available while exploring, including during movement. Each character stays at their current location. The camera eases over a short transition to the new leader while keeping its elevated angle. The previous leader becomes a follower, and held movement applies only to the new leader. Switching does not replay an action the player was already holding. A simultaneous switch and action performs the switch alone; a fresh action press is needed afterwards. The formation button and the orders follow the same rules: they work only while exploring, and pressed with the action they give their command alone. A switch ends the new leader's order; if the others are going or holding, the previous leader holds where they stand, and otherwise follows. The HUD shows the formation and what the squad is doing, such as "Close · following" or "Laura holding", and for a controller or keyboard the companions' fixed sides, "◀ Laura · Tom ▶".

Pause and dialogue suspend switching and world movement. Release guidance and fresh-input recovery apply after source changes, interruption, reconnection and pause transitions. On touch, lift every finger before continuing, even one resting at the stick centre. The stick and buttons can be held together; Pause keeps their contacts tracked while gameplay is frozen. The [party architecture](architecture.md) records command routing and ownership.

## Future squad and combat design

This section is design context, not a description of working features or a new implementation assignment. Exploration and salvage remain the intended main activities, with occasional tense combat. The [story bible](story/story-bible.md#combat-identities) gives Miki precision and disruption, Laura mobility and useful angles, and Tom protection and close-range stopping power. Their native weapons remain usable by the player on the bridge; signature abilities remain unimplemented. Autonomous companion contributions at the first guard are now assigned separately in the bounded revision above.

The retained Close, Spread and Column formations and move/hold/follow orders test readable group movement. They should serve an enjoyable loop rather than require extra controls to justify themselves. A future combat experiment can reconsider useful cover, coordinated fire or withdrawal once the core experience is agreed; the retired arena's presence in Git history does not commit the project to restoring it.

[The Quiet Yard](encounters/the-quiet-yard.md) and the story bible's later Relay House screenplay remain unimplemented narrative proposals. The Quiet Yard offers optional filter seals, a safe detour, a covered patrol gap, combat and retreat. It is retained to discuss the trio's roles and emotional purpose, not as an additional playable scene.

The preceding [bridge cover and spacing pass](bridge-cover-spacing.md) added two simple approach shelters and distinct intended companion places, preserving its damage comparison baseline. Its source and Simulator results are recorded in [PR #175](https://github.com/RonildoBraga/lauder/pull/175) and the [prototype plan](prototype-plan.md#bridge-cover-and-spacing--in-development). The current [eye/leg/grenade assignment](robot-eye-legs-grenades.md) builds on that source with the bounded changes above. Signature abilities, further places and broader combat systems remain separate decisions.
