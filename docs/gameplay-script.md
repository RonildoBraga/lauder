# Graybox duel — gameplay walkthrough

**Current experiment:** [issue #236](https://github.com/RonildoBraga/lauder/issues/236). The earlier bridge/story party is preserved at the [checkpoint](milestones/bridge-prototype.md). The anonymous hero here does not recast Miki or settle a new world/story.

The camera/focus follow-up is tracked in [issue #238](https://github.com/RonildoBraga/lauder/issues/238). The current readability work in [issue #241](https://github.com/RonildoBraga/lauder/issues/241) adds the projection comparison, bounded side approach, preparation warnings and free-slash aiming described below. These changes await fresh rendered checks and review; build48 is a candidate, not a delivery. The [prototype plan](prototype-plan.md#duel-readability-follow-up--in-progress) separates current validation from earlier source-bound evidence. Delivered TestFlight build47 predates both follow-ups.

You arrive in a small, open arena with a sword and shield. A single opponent waits ahead. You can immediately practise moving and turning; there are no conversations, equipment screens or long approaches before the fight.

The diagonal camera follows you, keeping the fighters upright and the floor directions clear. The current candidate uses a parallel orthographic view at the selected 35° pitch; the matched-scale perspective comparison remains available in Debug while we assess readability. Toggle **Focus** with Tab, R3/right-stick click or the touch button. A ring appears beneath the opponent and the camera frames the pair. You can strafe or walk backwards while turning towards the enemy, including behind your raised shield. Turning takes time; focus does not guarantee a block. Toggle again to move and turn freely. Without focus, Guard keeps your current facing.

As you approach, the enemy briefly moves towards a clear side position so the fighters are less likely to overlap in the camera. It commits to an attack after a short attempt rather than circling indefinitely. It raises the blade for a broad sweep, and a broad amber ground shape supplements the pose. The origin and direction commit before the attack; the warning does not keep aiming at you. You can back away, move around it, hold your shield towards it or time a short dodge. The pose and warning shape communicate the danger; colour is supplementary. The ground cue previews the whole attack's possible reach, not a guaranteed hit area, and disappears as the active swing starts.

If you block, your health stays intact but stamina drops. Keep holding guard and you will eventually exhaust it. Release guard and make space to regain stamina. A dodge has a vulnerable beginning and recovery, so pressing it early is not permanent protection.

The enemy finishes the sweep and visibly recovers. Aim with movement and attack: without Focus, a new slash takes the valid movement direction at the press, or current facing if there is no movement. The visible fighter aligns as the slash is accepted, then prepares, travels through its contact arc and follows through. Later movement cannot steer that swing. Focus instead uses your bounded facing towards the enemy and cannot steer an accepted slash or dodge into a new direction. One swing can damage the opponent once. An action pressed very late in recovery can buffer once; a free slash keeps the aim requested at that press, while a focused slash uses the current facing when accepted. Holding a button does not play an automatic combo.

The opponent next draws the weapon back for a longer-reaching thrust, with a narrow amber ground shape. Its longer preparation and narrower reach ask a different positioning question. Its direction remains committed, so a sidestep or retreat is useful. A successful defence earns another chance to respond.

There is no requirement to select a companion or match a weak point. The goal is to learn distance, timing and how much stamina to keep for the next response. Attack spam is a comparison case, not the intended best solution.

Health and stamina remain visible above the action. When either combatant falls, focus and attack warnings clear and the outcome panel offers an immediate Retry. Retry restores both fighters, resets the camera and consumes stale controls before the next attempt. Pause and app interruption freeze the current combat clock, hide the preparation cue and preserve focus selection. A resumed wind-up can show its still-committed warning again; returning does not reset the enemy into an unfair surprise or replay a held attack or focus press.

**What we need to learn:** can the player tell why a hit landed, choose a defence deliberately and want another attempt? Automated routes establish rule execution, not those subjective answers. If the duel fails that test, adjust it before adding parry, weapons, another enemy or new artwork.

A purposeful exploration route remains the next proposed experiment after combat: an optional useful find, a harder choice and a shortcut back. It is not part of this scene.
