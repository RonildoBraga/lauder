# Touch controls

The bridge can be played with the touch screen alone, for when no controller is at hand.

The [prototype plan](prototype-plan.md#touch-parity--in-development) owns its source checks and touch-only Simulator results. Delivered [build 36](prototype-plan.md#build-36-testflight-preview) remains the earlier baseline; this document does not establish phone acceptance of the follow-up.

The [robot contract](robot-eye-legs-grenades.md) defines aiming, flight, cover and blast damage. This source is not yet delivered; build 37 remains the phone comparison.

## Behaviour

Without an extended controller, show a fixed bottom-left analog stick plus **Look**, **Fire**, **Roll** beside Fire, **Swap: weapon** above Roll and Fire, **Next: name**, **Formation** and **Pause/Resume**. The three travel together from the start, so Next and Formation always show. With a controller attached, prefer it unless **Use touch controls** is selected; **Use controller** returns to it. The preference lasts for the run, not across launches.

Look makes the leader look around; it reads Continue during dialogue and Restart while paused. Fire uses the leader's weapon in hand at its pace ([ADR 0021](decisions/0021-paired-weapons.md)). Miki has a long rifle and an assault rifle, Laura a shotgun and an energy sword that she swings, and Tom a minigun and a shield dome that stays up while Fire is held. **Swap** brings the other weapon into hand and names it. Assistance turns the leader towards a clear target before firing; otherwise the shot follows their facing. Rounds are not counted beyond the magazine: magazines reload automatically, keep their state across switches and freeze on Pause. Practice outside a fight deals no hits.

Tap near an eligible enemy to [lock on](decisions/0020-lock-on.md), keeping the leader facing them while moving. A tap is down and up in less than **0.35 seconds**, with no more than **12 points** of drift, including the release position. At release, choose the nearest visible projected body envelope, using its full height and navigation radius with **30 screen points** of exterior allowance. This is a whole-body approximation, not a mesh or weak-point pick. Tapping another enemy changes the lock, and tapping the selected enemy again lets go. An empty valid tap retains an on-screen or partly visible lock but releases a locked enemy whose body is entirely off-screen; invalid taps or projection data do not release it. A tap does not fire. With no lock, [assisted aim](decisions/0007-aiming-and-shot-feedback.md) remains unchanged. Enemy taps are available while exploring even when the party cannot give position orders.

Roll makes the leader [dodge-roll](decisions/0019-dodge-roll.md) 3 m the way the stick points, or the way they face with the stick centred; hostile shots pass through them while they roll. It needs a fresh press, does not fire, and waits half a second after a roll before the next. Fire, Roll and Swap hide during dialogue as soon as any finger they own lifts; an existing capture remains until release so the neutral-input gate can still see it.

Next switches leadership once per press without changing positions. Formation cycles Close, Spread and Column; after a position order it reads Follow and calls companions back. Hold on the ground for about a third of a second to open the order menu, including over an enemy. Release on a visible choice to send Both or a named member, Hold, **Attack** or **Defend**. Away from the labels, the directional choices are left/right for companions, down for Hold and upper-left/upper-right for Attack/Defend; straight up in empty space cancels. An early lift issues no order and may be an enemy-selection tap under the rules above. Drifting more than about 12 points before the menu opens cancels the press. The camera ray fixes the marked ground point when the menu opens. An unreachable position destination gives a brief red cross; accepted destinations show each companion's ring. Attack/Defend change stance without needing a reachable point.

The [review follow-up](prototype-plan.md#claude-review-follow-ups--in-development) retains the original ground mark when the menu is clamped at an edge. Return within 30 points of the finger's start to restore Both. Beyond that reset area, the same rectangles that draw the visible choices select them, including Both; slides in gaps or outside the choices use the directional sectors from the finger's start. Straight up in empty space has a wider cancel sector. Near an edge, the 30-point reset takes priority even if a clamped label overlaps it. At the top edge, Attack/Defend labels may sit beside the finger, so horizontal travel onto them selects that stance. Starting a hold lower leaves room for upward cancellation; an extreme-top hold can still lack that space. These changes are included in delivered build 37; the newer Tom grenade control remains a separate revision.

The squad starts in Defend and the HUD names the current stance. Fire controls only the selected leader; it never grants companions permission to attack. After an explicit Attack order they may use their native roles against the guard. Defend stops companion attacks, cancels existing position orders and recalls them toward the leader using nearby legal cover. The stance persists across leader changes, while Retry/Restart returns to Defend. Position orders given afterward remain non-firing unless Attack is selected.

The maintainer chose a short contextual hint for finding squad orders. With touch ready, a revealed living guard and the squad in Defend, it explains holding the ground and the visible Attack/Defend choices; Pause and dialogue hide it. Gameplay buttons retain their positions.

Pause keeps Resume at the top right, Look reads Restart and Vibration switches On/Off. **Status panel: On/Off** beside Vibration also lets touch hide or show the HUD. When the panel is hidden, release guidance appears separately while input still needs to be released. There is no scene selector, test-weapon choice, Signature or separate Regroup action. Controller/keyboard equivalents are in the [input contract](prototype-plan.md#input-and-interaction-contract).

## Accessibility actions

Fire offers **Target**, **Next target** and **Let go** actions that change the lock without firing. Target keeps a valid selected enemy or acquires the native first target; Next target deliberately cycles without the controller's double-press release gesture. Formation offers **Squad Attack**, **Squad Defend**, `Send <name> ahead` for each companion, **Send both ahead** and **Hold positions**. Ahead orders use the leader's heading and the same reachable-destination checks as the controller commands; they do not select an arbitrary point by VoiceOver. These actions use the shared input rules and do not bypass session, order or release authority.

## Input ownership

TouchInput supplies raw values to GameControls and InputRules. It never calls session or world actions directly. Every control tracks its own finger, allowing movement beside a held Fire or tapped Next/Look. Quick taps latch until the next input sample. Enemy taps carry the current view size so their screen-point tolerance is independent of display resolution, and produce one targeting request per capture. Target requests share command priority with the other controls.

Source changes and inactivity cancel captures through generation tokens. Stale callbacks cannot affect a new capture. Pause and dialogue consume target requests instead of replaying them later. Pause leaves the capture surface mounted so every contact remains visible to the neutral-input gate. All fingers must lift before resuming, including a centred-stick finger. A press keeps the intent it began with until release, so an action held before Pause cannot turn into Restart. A valid lock survives a change between controller and touch; the new input source still requires neutral input.

The screen stays awake while Lauder is active in the foreground, including Pause, and restores normal idle locking when leaving.

## Checking

Earlier physical checks on iPhone 13 Pro Max verified simultaneous stick/button touches, one switch per press, stopping on release, Pause with held fingers and background recovery. They predate the bridge-only reset and do not establish its current behaviour. Shared rules and pointer-driven Simulator checks exercise presentation and routing; they do not establish multi-touch, controller handover, haptics or phone performance. Xbox hardware remains unverified.

The touch-parity route must use touch fields for movement, targeting, weapon/leader switching, Attack/Defend and Restart. Showing the touch layout over a controller-driven route does not establish those inputs. Source tests and a touch-only Simulator route do not establish real touch gestures, simultaneous fingers, VoiceOver usability or physical-phone acceptance. The extreme-top order-menu limitation above still needs an agreed phone check.

The [prototype plan](prototype-plan.md#deferred-phone-and-playtest-checks) records deferred physical checks. TestFlight availability is distinct from installation, playtesting and enjoyment. Use Simulator for daily iteration and perform a physical checkpoint when agreed.
